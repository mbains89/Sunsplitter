## 1. CANONICAL BACKGROUND SPINE

*Load-bearing for Fable and Build. Paragraphs 1–4 are canon. The Contested Layer Index below them is also canon — as a map of disputes, never as a resolution. No scene, ending, or record may confirm or eliminate a lane.*

**What the cascade was.** A planetary-defense standoff operation against a cataloged long-period object partially failed. The object fragmented late: the bulk missed, the fragment train did not. One arm loaded the upper atmosphere — hemispheric reentry firestorms, the burning sky every survivor account agrees on — and one arm threaded the orbital shells and started a runaway debris cascade that reached self-sustaining within hours. The official clock starts at fragmentation: hours to roughly two days of warning, and that is true as far as it goes. The private clock starts when the standoff operation was ordered: failure-mode projections existed inside agencies and contractors for months. Both clocks are accurate. The official story is not a lie; it is a choice of start time. That sentence is the engine of the no-clean-truth rule and must never be spoken aloud by any character as a conclusion.

**Why nine.** The ark was in mid-fit-out at an orbital yard inside the debris envelope. The habitat ring was unpressurized and uncrewed; only the core spine was boardable. The colonist complement — thousands — was staged groundside and station-side in scheduled waves that never came. Aboard on the last night was the commissioning skeleton shift, plus whoever cleared Tube 3 — the only mated transfer tube — before it sealed at 04:19:07 station time. Nine cleared: the Commander, the seven now living, and Rourke. Everyone at the hatch had a defensible reason to be standing there; whether the reasons reduce to schedule, luck, security decisions, or a name annotated on a list is precisely what the five accounts dispute. Rourke's version of that night died with him. The record is permanently one voice short, which is why it can never fully close.

**Why the ship launched incomplete and already damaged.** Cast-off came eleven hours into runaway, mid-loading-wave. The restart package — embryo vault, germplasm, data archive — was manifest tier one through three and already bolted down. The living-stores and spares lifters were tier four and never left the ground. Climb-out through thickening shells put eleven logged strikes into the ring and outer sections, plus an unlogged count, and forty-two of sixty-one ship systems flew without commissioning signatures. The dead sections were not killed by the cascade so much as never finished being born; the crew sealed them and stopped listening to them.

**Why scarcity from the first scene.** Thin stores are a launch schedule, not a math error. Integrity pressure is commissioning debt compounded by strike trauma. Every early scarcity or damage beat should be traceable to one of those two roots, cited by a character in their own vocabulary, never by system text. Nothing about the ship's condition requires malice. Everything about it requires hurry.

The Commander's own boarding stays unspecified: a player-shaped hole, permanently.

## 2. CREW BOARDING INSIGHTS

*One paragraph + spoken/private lines per living core character. Lines drafted to voice-card diction and reflex ownership (Lena who's hurt / Elias what's the threat / Mira what's broken / Jiro where are we / Tomas who pays / Amara who's owed / Sela what does it mean). Locked signature phrases untouched. Voice audit runs in a separate session per brief rule. Each insight names its lane and its later-surface hook.*

### Lena — the unfinished triage list *(lane: personal luck)*
Commissioning medical officer, certifying a med bay built for two hundred forty patients. She never chose the ship; she was mid-shift, too far in to get out. Through the tube glass she saw the station infirmary queue arrange itself into a mass-casualty drill that had no end-state. She carries the triage list she was working: forty-one names, no dispositions.
- "Ask me how I boarded and I'll show you a list with forty-one names and no dispositions. That's how. I was mid-sentence."
- "Nobody chose me. I was the wrong distance from the hatch in the right direction. Survival presented as proximity."
- "The queue on the station side looked like every drill I ever ran. The difference with a drill is it ends."
- *(private)* "I certified a med bay for two hundred forty. It certified. The paperwork survived. The paperwork always gets the last joke."
Later surface: the forty-one-name list is a physical object (What Remains candidate). In a late private moment she may finish exactly one disposition on it — one word — and say nothing.

### Elias — the seal at 04:19:07 *(lane: security decisions)*
He closed Tube 3. The countdown said ninety seconds; he sealed at eighty. The order arrived flagged from station command — and the debris log later showed station command was dead an hour before the flag. Either the order was relayed by someone unauthorized, or the system auto-issued it, or he read what he wanted to read. He does not know, and he is the only account of the accounts.
- "The countdown said ninety. I sealed at eighty. I have re-run the ten seconds I kept every night since."
- "There were faces in the tube glass. I knew four of them. That is the whole report."
- "Order came down flagged station command. Station command had been dead an hour by the debris log. So whose order did I follow. Standing question."
- "The seal held. That is what I am for."
Later surface: "whose order" is his private wound — it can price a late obedience/defiance beat (does he ever again execute an order he can't verify?) and is a natural ending scar if the Commander forces the question in public.

### Mira — the unsigned pages *(lane: earlier private projections)*
Commissioning engineer. She holds the log: sixty-one systems, nineteen signed, and every existing signature belongs to a dead engineer. She also noticed, before the sky, that the fit-out schedule compressed twice in the final month — change orders 4417 and 4491, justification fields empty. She has a gap where a reason should be, and she is constitutionally unable to fill gaps with speculation.
- "Sixty-one systems on the log. Nineteen signed. The signatures belong to dead engineers. I fly the other forty-two on faith, which is not a unit I work in."
- "Schedule compressed twice in the last month. Change orders 4417 and 4491. Justification field empty on both. That's not a conclusion. It's a gap where a reason should be."
- "The ring took eleven strikes we logged and an unknown count we didn't. Unknown is the part I maintain."
- *(private)* "I keep the log open to the unsigned pages. Closed, it's a record. Open, it's still a task list."
Later surface: the open log is a standing object in her spaces (What Remains candidate); the change-order numbers feed Scene Proposal B; her line about faith is a quiet rhyme with Sela that must never be pointed at.

### Tomas — tier four *(lane: manifest priority / who pays)*
He bolted the vault down himself and signed the load-out deviation, because mass margin said the vault or the second protein printer, and the alternative to signing was flying with neither. He carried seed crates through a crowd that wanted to be cargo. He has read the priority manifest. He knows what tier people were.
- "I bolted the vault down myself. Mass margin said the vault or the second printer. The vault doesn't eat. We do. Somebody signed that trade, and it was me."
- "The last two lifters carried the living stores. They never left the ground. So when you ask why the ration math is what it is — it isn't math. It's a launch schedule."
- "Priority manifest ran three tiers. Embryos. Seeds. Data. People were tier four."
- *(private)* "I carried seed crates past a crowd that wanted to be cargo. Both hands full. That's my alibi."
Later surface: "People were tier four." is a minted single-owner phrase (Grok lock, §4) — it should recur at most once, late, in a Living-aligned reckon or ending, in his mouth only. His signature on the deviation is a debt object: the trade that made him is the trade he keeps re-litigating in the trays.

### Amara — the list in her hands *(lane: political selection)*
The manifest was physically in her hands when the alarm sounded; that is the entire mechanism of her survival, and she knows how it sounds. Two hundred fourteen confirmed berths. Some names carry annotation codes she was never taught — sponsor codes, triage codes, somebody's shorthand. She has decided not to decide, because the day she interprets those codes, she starts assigning blame among the dead.
- "The manifest was in my hands when the alarm sounded. That's the whole story of how I boarded. I was holding the list."
- "Two hundred fourteen confirmed berths. Some names have codes beside them I was never taught. Sponsor codes. Triage codes. Somebody's shorthand. I've decided not to decide."
- "Everyone aboard is owed by someone who isn't. I keep that ledger too. Nobody has asked to see it."
- *(private)* "Their columns stay on the board. That isn't sentiment. That's the debt staying legible."
Later surface: the manifest tablet is the anchor of Scene Proposal A. Her refusal-to-interpret is the political lane's containment vessel: if the Commander reads the list, the lane gets louder in the crew's mouths, but never resolves.

### Jiro — the file from June *(lane: earlier private projections, independent axis)*
He was aboard because alignment sims run at night; the sky picked the night shift. The departure burn they actually flew was not improvised — it was a contingency file computed nine weeks before the cascade, requested by someone whose name is not in the file header. He can state the ship's position to the meter for every second of that night. Cause is not his column.
- "Tube sealed at 04:19:07 station time. We were four hundred twelve kilometers into the climb-out corridor. I can give you position to the meter for every second of that day. Cause is not my column."
- "The burn we flew was computed nine weeks before the cascade. Contingency file. Somebody asked for it in June. I've stopped asking why out loud."
- "I was aboard because alignment sims run at night. That's all it is. The sky picked the night shift."
- *(private)* "Every chart I own still measures from Sol. So does the file that saved us."
Later surface: the contingency file's request timestamp is the projections lane's second, independent artifact. It may sit near Mira's change orders in the record; the two must never be synthesized on screen (Index rule). His "stopped asking out loud" prices a late private beat where the Commander can invite him to ask.

### Sela — the hand-off that never happened *(lane: personal luck, refused)*
She carried the congregation's consecrated crate through Tube 3 for delivery. She was meant to hand it off and walk back to the station side. The tube closed. The hand-off did not occur; she became it. Her survival is clerical — a crate, a schedule, a door — and she has refused both available framings of that fact, because luck insults the dead and selection flatters her.
- "I carried the congregation's crate through the tube, and the tube closed. I was meant to hand it off and walk back. The hand-off did not occur. I am the hand-off."
- "They will say luck, or they will say selection. I decline both. Something was asked of me, and I have not yet been told what."
- "The register I carry holds more names than this ship had berths. I am its last legible line."
- *(private)* "If it was clerical — a crate, a schedule, a door — then meaning is a thing I must build rather than find. I have decided to find that acceptable. Slowly."
Later surface: "I am the hand-off." is a minted single-owner phrase (Grok lock, §4). The undelivered crate is already seeded in canon (the filter crate stenciled for a congregation); this insight gives that object its origin without touching the locked lamp-ritual prose. Her lane-refusal is ending-grade: whether she ever accepts a framing can shade her branch of the late meaning beats.

*(Vess: excluded per request — Dawnbreak origin, separate package. Rourke: no insight authored; his missing account is load-bearing absence per Spine ¶2 and must stay unwritten.)*

## 3. SCENE PROPOSALS (2 — both optional, both pure-data)

### Proposal A — `boarding_manifest` (new scene: "The List She Holds")

```
// ============================================================
// SCENE: boarding_manifest
// PRECONDITIONS (live predicates, evaluated at render):
//   isAlive("amara") for the full scene;
//   Amara-dead variant fires instead if !isAlive("amara") && amara boarded-effects reachable
//   !state.marks.manifest   (one-shot)
//   Anchor: interstitial slot adjacent to empty_berths visit or the
//   quiet/observation hub — exact spine anchor = Grok/Build bind (Open Q3)
// STATE WRITES (destination onEnter only):
//   state.marks.manifest = "read" | "declined"
//   RUN_FACT on "read":   "read the boarding manifest — two hundred fourteen names"
//   RUN_FACT on "declined": "let the manifest stay closed"
// DEATH EXPOSURE: none (no death vector in scene)
// DEAD-SPEECH: Amara-dead variant is silent-object only — the tablet in her
//   effects, no interpretation, no codes beat, same two choices, same writes
// IMAGE: reuse empty_berths (interim). ART_REQUESTS note: "manifest_tablet"
//   close-up plate, low priority, non-blocking
// ============================================================
```
**Shape.** Amara is at the board. If asked about the boarding — or if the Commander lingers at the berths — she offers the tablet, face down. Choice: read the names / don't. If read: the 214 scroll past; the annotation codes appear exactly once, undefined; a third beat lets the Commander ask about the codes, and Amara's answer is her "decided not to decide" line — the ask writes nothing extra, it only spends the moment.
**Feeds.** Political-selection lane gains a witnessed artifact without gaining a verdict. Downstream: existing reckon / cascade-account scenes may condition one additional crew-cited line on `marks.manifest == "read"` (the crew knows the Commander has read it; the argument gets a new exhibit, not a new conclusion). departure_truth flags untouched.
**Why it earns its bytes.** It converts the political lane from narration into a choice the player made with their hands — reading the dead's names or refusing to — and gives Amara's confessor texture its origin object. "Let the manifest stay closed" is a What Remains line that costs nothing and lands hard. Attachment and consequence, zero new systems.

### Proposal B — `cascade_records` expansion ("The Unsigned Pages") — expansion, not a new scene

```
// ============================================================
// SCENE: cascade_records (EXPANSION — adds one gated beat)
// PRECONDITIONS (live predicates):

// STATE WRITES (destination onEnter only):
//   state.marks.changeorders = "logged" | "buried"

//   RUN_FACT on "buried": "kept change orders 4417 and 4491 out of the record"
// DEATH EXPOSURE: none
// DEAD-SPEECH: n/a (beat gated on isAlive("mira"); base scene unchanged)
// IMAGE: reuse cascade_records (current plate flagged in Art punch-list —
//   this expansion adds no image dependency and does not block on the regen)
// ============================================================
```
**Shape.** Inside the existing records scene, Mira surfaces the change orders — her gap-where-a-reason-should-be line — and puts the decision where it belongs: enter the finding into the ship's official record, or leave it out. She will do either without protest. Logging it doesn't prove anyone knew; it proves the schedule compressed, and it changes which account the crew argues *from* in later record and reckon beats (one conditioned line each, `marks.changeorders == "logged"`). Burying it creates a private two-person fact: she knows the Commander knows.
**Feeds.** Projections lane gains its first player fingerprint. Plurality preserved structurally: the artifact supports the lane and cannot close it — the justification fields are empty, and empty is not evidence of anything except emptiness.
**Why it earns its bytes.** The cascade backstory stops being lore the player receives and becomes a record the player curates. "Buried" is a slow scar with no meter: it surfaces only through Mira's withheld speech in later private beats, exactly the private-contradiction pattern the creative standard mandates.
